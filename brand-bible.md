## The origin story

You started baking at age 15 in Tunisia. Every Sunday — your one day off school — you'd bake for your family. Mostly cookies. They still remember those original cookies. The Sunday tradition traveled with you from Tunisia to Cologne, and twelve years later, that's still where these cookies come from.

**The brand is dedicated to your dad** — the person who first looked forward to your Sunday baking, and whose love made the ritual real. (You always made him quieter, lower-sugar versions at home. The commercial cookies are full-sugar NY-style — chewy, gooey, indulgent — because that's what the cookie genre demands. The dedication to him lives in the story and the ritual, not in the recipe.)

## Brand pillars (in priority order)

1. **A Sunday ritual.** Bake Friday → ship Saturday → eat Sunday. The brand IS the cadence.
2. **Big, chewy, gooey — no compromise.** NY-style done right. Indulgence is the point. Rich brown butter, deep brown sugar, real chocolate. No "lighter" version for the public — that's a different brand.
3. **One pair of hands.** Made weekly by one person, in one kitchen, in limited drops. Scarcity is honest, not manufactured.
4. **Dedicated to your dad.** The Sunday tradition started with him. Every box, in spirit, is for him.

## Voice & tone

**The brand sounds like:** an old friend writing you a Sunday letter. Warm but never cute. Quietly confident. Lets the product speak.

**Do:**
- First person, casually ("I baked you the lemon raspberry this week")
- Sensory language — warm, gooey, fragrant, golden, soft
- Reference Sundays often
- Be honest about the small-batch, one-person nature
- Use lowercase casually on social — feels like a text from a friend

**Don't:**
- "Indulge in our heavenly cookies!" / exclamation marks / corporate sweet-bakery cliché
- "Artisanal," "hand-crafted," "small-batch" — tired words
- Performed Tunisian culture or generic Middle Eastern aesthetics
- Aggressive sales language, "LAST CHANCE" caps
- Cute mascots, googly-eyed cookies, pastel-cartoon visuals

**Sample voice:**
> Sunday No. 14. Three cookies in this week's letter — the chocolate chip is using a new dark chocolate from Belgium that I'm slightly in love with. The caramel pecan now has flaky salt on top because I couldn't help myself. Boxes ship Saturday. — A

## Color palette

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Aubergine (primary dark)** | `#2A1A2A` | Backgrounds, packaging base, dark mode site |
| **Cream (primary light)** | `#F5E8D0` | Type on dark, packaging accents, parchment feel |
| **Caramel (warm accent)** | `#A56B3A` | Cookie illustration color, golden details |
| **Lilac (signature)** | `#C8B5D5` | Sparing accent — ribbon, link color, brand signature |

Always lean **dark + warm**. Never bright/saturated. Never pastel.

## Typography

- **Wordmark / Display:** Cormorant Garamond (Google Fonts, free)
- **Body / UI:** Inter or Manrope
- **Personal touch:** your actual handwriting (scanned) for sign-offs ("— A") and the note in each box

Launch with ONE. Add others as their recipes are perfected — each becomes a moment.

- **The Sunday** (chocolate chip) — Day 1 hero. The 12-year recipe. The dad's cookie. ("The Sunday" inside "The Sunday Cookie" — recursive, ritualistic.)
- Caramel pecan — coming when ready
- Lemon raspberry white chocolate — coming when ready

**Real quote to use prominently:**
> "My mom doesn't even like chocolate. She still asks for these every Sunday."

## Dad dedication — how to do it

Public but quiet. Three real applications:

- Website footer, every page: *"for my dad — A."* in small italic Cormorant
- Product page bottom: one italic line, *"Dedicated to my dad."*
- Box notes occasionally — never every box, never loud

Never: hero banners, big headlines, "DEDICATED TO MY DAD" treatments. The dedication should reward attention, not demand it.

## What we are NOT

- Not Crumbl. Not aggressively sweet, not flavor-of-the-week, not loud.
- Not a Levain knockoff. Refined, quieter.
- Not a "bakery." A Sunday letter that happens to be cookies.
- Not corporate. There's one person making these. Always.
